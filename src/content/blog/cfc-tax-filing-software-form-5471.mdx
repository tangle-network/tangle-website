Leah owns 60% of a software company incorporated outside the United States.
She received a bank statement, a shareholder register, a translated income statement, and a prior-year filing record, but the ownership changed twice during the year.
She asks controlled foreign corporation (CFC) tax filing software to “prepare Form 5471,” yet the first question is not which Portable Document Format (PDF) file to fill.
It is which facts establish her relationship to the foreign corporation during the relevant period.

CFC tax filing software for Form 5471 should build that ownership timeline, collect the entity’s financial records, explain which schedules appear in the draft, and show what remains unresolved.
It can organize a high-friction preparation job.
The final filing category still requires the full fact pattern and review.

This article describes a preparation and review workflow, not tax advice.
Controlled foreign corporation reporting is fact-specific and often deserves professional review.

## A CFC analysis needs an ownership timeline

A controlled foreign corporation (CFC) is a foreign corporation controlled by U.S. shareholders under the federal rules.
A U.S. shareholder is a person treated as a U.S. person for federal tax purposes who meets the ownership threshold for the applicable rule.
At a high level, the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) describes control as more than 50% of the voting power or value of the stock being owned directly, indirectly, or constructively by U.S. shareholders.
For several Form 5471 categories, the instructions use a 10% threshold for a U.S. person’s voting power or value interest.
The relevant threshold and filing category depend on the facts.
Constructive ownership means that attribution rules can treat another person's shares as belonging to the taxpayer.

Those thresholds are a starting explanation, not a filing conclusion.
Attribution rules, ownership changes, filer categories, entity classification, and the corporation’s tax year can alter the analysis.

The [IRS CFC metadata page](https://www.irs.gov/statistics/soi-tax-stats-controlled-foreign-corporations-metadata) explains the control concept.
The [Form 5471 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5471) define the filer categories and say the requirements must be read category by category.
The [Form 5471 page](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-5471) says certain U.S. citizens and residents who are officers, directors, or shareholders in certain foreign corporations file Form 5471 and schedules.
For the general foreign-corporation tax discussion, read [Controlled Foreign Corporation Taxes And Form 5471](/blog/controlled-foreign-corporation-taxes-form-5471), and for the wider source-to-review workflow, read [AI Tax Preparation For Complex Returns](/blog/ai-tax-preparation-complex-returns).

Software should present those sources beside the facts that triggered a question.
It should avoid the shortcut “foreign ownership equals Form 5471.”

## Start with the ownership timeline

A workpaper is a record that shows how a reported value or filing input was assembled.
The first workpaper should show who owned what, when, and through which path.
An end-of-year cap table is insufficient when shares were issued, transferred, redeemed, or held through another entity.

Leah’s illustrative timeline might look like this:

| Date | Event | Source | Review state |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| January 1 | Leah owns 55% of voting shares | prior-year register | candidate |
| April 15 | new investor receives 20% | financing agreement, page 12 | needs-review |
| April 15 | Leah’s voting rights change under a side letter | side letter, page 3 | needs-review |
| September 30 | Leah buys additional non-voting shares | purchase agreement, page 8 | needs-review |
| December 31 | year-end register reports 60% value interest | corporation statement | candidate |

The system should not replace this with “Leah owns 60%.”
The annual percentage may be relevant, but the path to that percentage determines which facts need review.

The timeline should capture:

- direct, indirect, and constructive ownership paths;
- voting power and value separately;
- acquisition and disposition dates;
- classes of stock and voting rights;
- officer or director roles;
- the foreign corporation’s annual accounting period;
- related entities and agreements;
- prior-year positions and corrections.

The last item is often the one software loses.
Form 5471 work can depend on facts carried from earlier periods, so the current draft must remain connected to prior workpapers.

## Form 5471 is a package of schedule questions

Form 5471 is an information return with schedules.
The schedule set depends on the filer category and the foreign corporation’s facts.
In this context, a filer category is the IRS category that determines which information and schedules a person reports.

The IRS page lists schedule areas including taxes paid, earnings and profits, related-party transactions, stock organization changes, previously taxed earnings and profits, CFC income groups, and distributions.
The software should show why a schedule is present, absent, or blocked.
Earnings and profits (E&P) is a tax measure used to track a corporation’s earnings for distribution and inclusion rules.
Previously taxed earnings and profits (PTEP) means earnings already included in a prior United States tax calculation that may matter when later distributed.

| Schedule area | Facts the workpaper should connect | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| taxes paid or accrued | foreign tax records and payment dates | what source supports the amount and period? |
| current earnings and profits | local accounts and U.S. tax adjustments | which accounting records support the draft? |
| accumulated earnings and profits | prior schedules and distributions | does the opening balance reconcile? |
| CFC income groups | income, deductions, assets, and tax data | are the required categories supported? |
| related-party transactions | loans, payments, purchases, and transfers | are all related persons and periods included? |
| organization or stock changes | formations, reorganizations, acquisitions, and dispositions | did the ownership timeline change the schedule? |
| previously taxed earnings and profits (PTEP) | prior-year PTEP workpapers and distributions | does the current distribution history tie out? |
| distributions | shareholder payments and dates | which records support each distribution? |

Use the table as a preparation map; use the IRS page and instructions for schedule selection.
The [current IRS Form 5471 page](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-5471) and its linked instructions remain the source of the filing requirements.

## Currency and period belong in each number

Foreign records often arrive in a currency and accounting period that do not match the United States tax workpaper.
Functional currency is the currency used for the corporation’s accounting records, and it may differ from the currency used in the U.S. workpaper.
The conversion is part of the evidence, not a cosmetic formatting step.

For a sample calculation, suppose a receipt records €80,000 of foreign tax paid on a date when the documented exchange rate is 1.08 United States dollars per euro.
The workpaper can show $86,400 as a candidate translated amount because €80,000 multiplied by 1.08 equals $86,400.
It must also retain the rate source, the date, the tax type, and the payer.

| Field | Candidate value | What remains to review |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| local amount | €80,000 | receipt and assessment |
| conversion rate | 1.08 USD per euro | source and applicable date |
| translated candidate | $86,400 | period, tax type, and payer |

If the software uses a current spot rate without recording why, the number cannot be reproduced later.
If the receipt covers a different period from the corporation’s accounting records, the arithmetic can be correct while the workpaper is still wrong.
The product should keep the candidate visible and stop the schedule until those joins are resolved.

## A worked example shows why the facts stay separate

In this sample, Leah’s foreign corporation reports 400,000 units of local-currency revenue, €80,000 of foreign taxes, one shareholder loan, and a distribution in December.
The software should not turn those values directly into a filing position.
It should assemble the evidence and identify the joins:

| Fact | Source | Join needed before drafting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| revenue and expenses | foreign income statement | map accounting categories to the relevant U.S. tax workpaper |
| foreign taxes | tax payment receipt and assessment | match tax type, period, currency, and payer |
| shareholder loan | loan agreement and bank ledger | identify related parties, terms, dates, and balances |
| distribution | board resolution and bank statement | connect payment to ownership and earnings records |
| ownership | register, agreements, and prior filings | determine the relevant filer categories and periods |

If the local accounts use euros and the tax workpaper uses U.S. dollars, the conversion rate and date must be recorded.
If the distribution ledger and bank statement disagree, the software should flag the difference.
If the prior-year PTEP schedule is missing, the current distribution work should remain open.

The right output is a question list such as:

```text
1. Confirm whether the April side letter changed voting power or only economic rights.
2. Provide the missing prior-year PTEP workpaper before finalizing distribution treatment.
3. Confirm the tax period and currency conversion source for the €80,000 foreign tax amount.
4. Reconcile the shareholder-loan balance to the year-end ledger.
```

Those questions are more valuable than a draft that hides its assumptions.

## A source-linked model keeps the form explainable

The following is illustrative TypeScript for an application model, not a Tangle SDK type.
The workpaper can represent facts without pretending they are final:

```typescript
type FactState = 'candidate' | 'needs-review' | 'accepted' | 'blocked'

type CfcFact = {
  field: string
  value: string | number
  unit?: string
  period: string
  source?: {
    document: string
    page?: number
    excerpt: string
  }
  state: FactState
  affects: string[]
}

const ownershipFact: CfcFact = {
  field: 'voting-interest',
  value: 0.55,
  unit: 'fraction',
  period: '2025-01-01/2025-04-14',
  source: {
    document: 'share-register.pdf',
    page: 1,
    excerpt: 'Leah Morgan: 55% voting shares',
  },
  state: 'needs-review',
  affects: ['ownership timeline', 'Form 5471 category review'],
}

console.log(ownershipFact)
```

The `period` field prevents a single year-end value from erasing an earlier change.
The `affects` field tells a reviewer why the fact matters.
The `state` field makes it impossible to confuse a document extraction with a final decision.

## Prior years are part of the current work

The current-year workpaper depends on prior-year records.
Opening earnings and profits, prior distributions, prior classifications, and prior filings can affect what the current reviewer needs to understand.

The product should carry forward:

| Prior-year artifact | Current-year use |
| --- | --- |
| ownership register | compare changes and effective dates |
| Form 5471 schedules | explain recurring facts and changed schedules |
| earnings and profits workpaper | establish opening balances |
| PTEP records | connect prior inclusions and current distributions |
| foreign tax workpaper | compare taxes by type, period, and currency |
| correction log | show why current values differ from last year |

Carry-forward requires re-confirming each prior value.
Mark every prior value as inherited, re-confirmed, corrected, or superseded.

## Stop conditions should be part of the product

CFC tax filing software should block or escalate when:

| Stop condition | Why the draft must pause |
| --- | --- |
| ownership changes without effective dates | the relevant period and category analysis may be wrong |
| voting power and value percentages differ | one percentage cannot stand in for both |
| direct and indirect ownership paths are unclear | attribution may change the facts under review |
| officer or director status is unconfirmed | the role may affect the filing analysis |
| prior listed Form 5471 records are missing | current schedules may depend on prior positions |
| foreign financial statements are incomplete | income, assets, taxes, and earnings workpapers lack support |
| functional currency is unclear | translated amounts cannot be reproduced |
| related-party transactions are not documented | schedule and disclosure questions remain open |
| foreign taxes have no period or payment support | the amount cannot be safely carried into a workpaper |

The product should use precise messages.
“Ownership changed on April 15, but the side letter does not identify whether voting power changed” is a review question.
“CFC risk detected” is a label without a recovery path.

## The advisor handoff is part of the outcome

If a taxpayer starts with CFC tax filing software, the advisor should receive one coherent packet:

- ownership timeline with direct, indirect, and constructive paths identified;
- foreign entity profile and accounting period;
- source index with document status and page references;
- schedule checklist with the reason each item is present or open;
- earnings and profits, tax, distribution, and related-party workpapers;
- prior-year comparison and correction log;
- missing-fact list with the question each item would answer;
- draft Form 5471 package marked unapproved.

The unresolved list should be exportable.
It should name the missing financial statement, ownership date, currency conversion record, prior schedule, or transaction agreement instead of asking an advisor to search through a chat transcript.

## What the software cannot decide

Software can organize a filing analysis.
It cannot prove that the taxpayer supplied every relevant fact, that an entity is classified correctly for every purpose, or that a disputed legal position is acceptable.

Form 5471 has several filer categories and schedules.
The correct category depends on facts the software must collect and the taxpayer or advisor must evaluate.
The [IRS instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5471) should remain linked to the workpaper rather than summarized as a universal rule.

The product should also avoid conflating Form 5471 with other international forms.
Depending on the facts, reviewers may need to consider [Form 8858](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8858), [Form 8938](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8938), [Form 8621](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8621), or the IRS [foreign tax credit guidance](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-tax-credit).
The workflow can surface those questions.
It should not infer the final form set from the presence of one foreign document.

## Choose software that can explain the schedule path

Use CFC tax filing software for Form 5471 only when it can show the ownership timeline, source documents, period and currency details, schedule reasoning, prior-year dependencies, open questions, and approval state.
If it produces a completed-looking form without those workpapers, keep the output in draft and send the records to a qualified reviewer.

For a foreign corporation, choose the system that makes the ownership timeline, source evidence, schedule reasoning, open questions, and approval state visible before it makes the PDF look complete.
For pass-through basis and entity reconciliation, see [S corporation tax software for basis and K-1s](/blog/s-corp-tax-software-basis-k1).


## What is CFC tax filing software?

CFC tax filing software organizes controlled foreign corporation facts, foreign financial records, Form 5471 workpapers, schedules, and review questions.

## Does owning a foreign company always require Form 5471?

No.
The requirement depends on specific facts and filer categories, so software should collect the facts and link the relevant IRS instructions instead of guessing.

## What should CFC tax software collect?

It should collect ownership history, voting and value interests, officer or director roles, entity periods, financial statements, taxes, distributions, related-party transactions, prior filings, and source documents.

## Can AI prepare Form 5471?

AI can extract and organize records and draft workpapers.
The filing category, schedules, calculations, and final position still need careful taxpayer or professional review.

## Can Tangle prove a Form 5471 package is correct?

No.
Tangle can provide infrastructure patterns for a recorded runtime, operator-run service, payment path, and execution evidence.
Correctness still depends on the tax facts, sources, rules, and review decision.
