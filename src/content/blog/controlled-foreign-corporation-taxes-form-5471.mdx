Sam, a U.S. founder, held 55% of the voting power in Atlas Ltd, a foreign software company, on January 1, 2025.
He sold some shares in March.
By December, Atlas had paid local corporate tax but no dividend, and Sam directly held 40% of its voting power.
He asks whether the missing dividend means there is no U.S. filing or tax issue.

**Does owning a foreign company always create a controlled foreign corporation tax bill? No.**
Form 5471 reporting and a U.S. income inclusion are separate questions.
Form 5471 is an information return that certain U.S. persons attach to their tax returns.
Some U.S. shareholders may have a subpart F or section 951A income inclusion without receiving a dividend.
The [IRS Form 5471 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5471) describe the filing categories and the schedules used for those income questions.
This article follows the records and review decisions; it does not calculate Sam's tax.

For this example, assume Atlas uses a calendar tax year, is a foreign corporation for U.S. tax purposes, and has no U.S. owners other than Sam and the holding company below, subject to reviewer confirmation.
A **controlled foreign corporation** (CFC) generally has U.S. shareholders who collectively own more than 50% of its voting power or value on any day of its tax year.
A **U.S. shareholder** is a U.S. person, which includes a domestic company, that generally owns at least 10% of voting power or value under the applicable direct, indirect, and constructive ownership rules.
The [IRS definitions and exceptions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5471) matter more than a year-end percentage alone.

**Tangle Tax Agent** is a proposed workflow that would collect records, draft workpapers, and stop for taxpayer or advisor review.
It would not be a public filing engine or the source of final tax opinions.

## Sam's January ownership keeps Form 5471 in view

The [Category 4 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5471) describe control as more than 50% of voting power or value at any time during the person's tax year.
Sam's January 55% voting interest therefore raises a Category 4 filing question even though he later sold shares.
The reviewer still must confirm Atlas's U.S. tax classification, the stock records, the relevant tax years, and any filing exception.
The December 40% field cannot close that question.

## Records to collect across Sam's year

- **January 1:** Sam directly holds 55% of Atlas's voting power and serves as a director.
  Keep the cap table, stock rights, and director record.
- **March 15:** Sam sells shares carrying 15 percentage points of voting power to an unrelated investor.
  Keep the signed sale agreement and updated cap table.
- **June 30:** A domestic holding company (HoldCo) in which Sam has an interest buys stock carrying 12% of Atlas's voting power.
  Get its purchase agreement and ownership records.
- **September 10:** Atlas lends money to Sam's U.S. company.
  Keep the loan agreement, ledger entries, and interest records.
- **December 31:** Atlas reports income and local tax in euros, with no distribution shown.
  Keep the annual accounts, tax receipt, bank records, and prior Form 5471 workpapers.

The June purchase does not establish how much of HoldCo's stock is attributed to Sam.

## Test both thresholds on every ownership date

A Tangle sandbox is a Linux machine created for one job and deleted afterward.
The quickstart creates one, runs a command with `box.exec`, and deletes it.

<Example path="sandboxes/run-code" />

Inside its `try` block, replace the `box.exec` call with this check over the cap table on each date the ownership changed:

```ts
await box.write("/home/agent/cfc_test.py", `
# Each block of Atlas voting power: (%, U.S. persons who own it directly or by attribution).
cap_table = {
    "Jan 1": [(55, ["Sam"]), (45, [])],
    "Mar 15, after the sale": [(40, ["Sam"]), (60, [])],
    "Jun 30": [(40, ["Sam"]), (12, ["HoldCo"]), (48, [])],
    "Jun 30, HoldCo stock attributed to Sam": [(40, ["Sam"]), (12, ["HoldCo", "Sam"]), (48, [])],
}

def cfc_test(blocks):
    owned = {}
    for pct, owners in blocks:
        for owner in owners:
            owned[owner] = owned.get(owner, 0) + pct
    shareholders = {o for o, pct in owned.items() if pct >= 10}  # U.S. shareholder: 10% or more
    held = sum(pct for pct, owners in blocks if shareholders & set(owners))  # each block once
    return owned, held

for day, blocks in cap_table.items():
    owned, held = cfc_test(blocks)
    print(f"{day}: {owned}, U.S. shareholders hold {held}%,", "CFC" if held > 50 else "not a CFC")
`);
const { exitCode, stdout } = await box.exec("python3 /home/agent/cfc_test.py");
console.log(exitCode, stdout);
```

The script exits 0 and prints:

```text
Jan 1: {'Sam': 55}, U.S. shareholders hold 55%, CFC
Mar 15, after the sale: {'Sam': 40}, U.S. shareholders hold 40%, not a CFC
Jun 30: {'Sam': 40, 'HoldCo': 12}, U.S. shareholders hold 52%, CFC
Jun 30, HoldCo stock attributed to Sam: {'Sam': 52, 'HoldCo': 12}, U.S. shareholders hold 52%, CFC
```

HoldCo's 12% makes it a U.S. shareholder, so Atlas tests as a CFC again from June 30.
Attribution moves Sam's own percentage from 40% to as much as 52%, but each block counts once, so Atlas's total stays 52%.
The script takes attribution as a reviewer input and tests voting power only; value needs the same test.

Category 5 asks a different question.
Under the [IRS Category 5 rules](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5471), the reviewer examines CFC status during the year and ownership on the last day Atlas was a CFC.
On these inputs that day is December 31, when Sam directly held 40%.
Neither category by itself calculates Sam's income inclusion or tax due.
The immediate decision is to keep Categories 4 and 5 as filing candidates and to check the June chain and applicable exceptions.

## Which Form 5471 schedules belong in Sam's packet?

The [IRS filing chart](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5471) also says a required schedule may need to be filed when all its amounts are zero.
Sam's statement that Atlas paid no dividend is therefore a fact to verify, not a reason to omit every distribution question.

- **Schedule O, stock changes:** Use the March sale and June purchase agreements to confirm dates, parties, and stock interests.
- **Schedule M, related-party transactions:** Use the September loan agreement and ledger to review the parties and reportable loan details.
- **Schedule E, foreign taxes:** Match the local tax return to the receipt and currency conversion.
  Assess credit rules separately.
- **Schedules H, J, and P, earnings history:** Build current earnings and profits (E&P) from tax-adjusted accounts.
  Reconcile accumulated E&P to the prior Schedule J.
  Carry forward previously taxed E&P (PTEP) only from supported inclusion and distribution records.
- **Schedules I, I-1, and Q, income information:** Use tax-adjusted income and shareholder records for the subpart F and section 951A review.
  Classify income, deductions, taxes, and assets by the applicable CFC income groups.
- **Schedule R, distributions:** Check bank and shareholder records against Sam's no-distribution statement, then follow the filing chart.

The [IRS schedule descriptions](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-5471) identify each schedule's purpose.
They do not turn a foreign trial balance into U.S. tax-adjusted E&P.
Schedule H reports current E&P for U.S. tax purposes, while Schedule J and Schedule P carry different histories.
If Atlas's earlier workpapers are missing, the packet should show that gap instead of inventing an opening balance.

Sam's local tax receipt is an input, not a credit decision.
The [IRS foreign tax credit guidance](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-tax-credit) explains that eligibility and limitations must be assessed.
Keep the tax amount, payment evidence, currency conversion, and credit question together.

## Keep each translation and assumption visible

The [Form 5471 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5471) specify when amounts appear in dollars, functional currency, or both.
For each Atlas amount, keep the original euros, accounting period, exchange rate and source, converted dollars, and review state.
A rate used for one schedule should not silently fill another schedule.

An extracted number alone cannot explain why Sam's filing category or E&P balance changed.

## Stop the draft where Sam's records stop

The workflow ends with a proposed packet: draft schedules, open questions, and the sources behind each.
Nothing counts as submitted until a reviewer approves the proposal.

Sam should ask a qualified reviewer to resolve the filing categories, schedules, and U.S. income questions against the applicable year's rules before filing.
If those records and calculations are already reconciled, conventional tax software with manual Form 5471 review may be enough.
